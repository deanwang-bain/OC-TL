Executive summary · Nike, Inc.
A defensible, quantifiable margin opportunity
  • Nike's Gross Margin trails the peer median by 7.4 points, the core driver of the profitability gap
    • Gross margin sits at 44.6% of revenue against a peer median of 52.0% — the widest gap of any profitability metric we benchmarked
    • SG&A efficiency partly offsets this: at 34.2% of revenue, Nike runs 7.3pp better than the peer median of 41.5%, even after growing 7.8% a year against 1.2% revenue growth
  • EBITDA margin still leads peers today — a cushion, not a solution
    • Nike's EBITDA margin of 12.4% sits 4.1 percentage points above the peer median of 8.3%
    • That outperformance is masking the Gross Margin problem, and won't persist if peers close their own cost gaps first
  • We see a defensible path to close the gap across four areas
    • (A) Commercial excellence — segment & volume growth, share of wallet, pricing & margin management
    • (B) Operational excellence — conversion efficiency, direct materials, freight & product cost engineering
    • (C) SG&A efficiency — organisation & headcount, indirect spend, IT rationalisation
    • (D) Capital efficiency — working capital and capital project discipline, a one-off cash release
  • Across all four areas, we see $305M–$650M of recurring EBITDA impact, plus $71M–$143M of one-off cash — a credible path to turn today's cushion into structural strength
    • The two figures are shown separately because they are not the same currency — recurring P&L impact and one-off balance-sheet cash are never summed into one headline number
    • We recommend starting with SG&A Efficiency and Commercial Excellence, which together carry roughly three-quarters of the recurring P&L opportunity and close the Gross Margin gap fastest

What "recommended range" means
Each bucket's range reconciles two independent estimates: Bain exp., built bottom-up from Bain's experience running similar programmes at comparable apparel/footwear companies, and Peer bench., the gap implied by closing some or all of the distance to the peer median on the same metric (shown only where enough peers disclose comparable data). The recommended range is the overlap between the two — wide enough to be realistic, narrow enough to commit to.
In cost-reduction terms, the $305M–$650M P&L range equals roughly 0.7–1.4% of Nike's combined COGS and SG&A base ($45.9B), or 0.6–1.3pp of recurring EBITDA margin. The $71M–$143M cash range equals roughly 6–13 days of Nike's 8-day NWC gap to peer median being closed.

Methodology · appendix
Four principles keep this defensible
General approach and metric definitions used to assess Nike's margin and cash opportunity
Every ratio is benchmarked on an adjusted basis, one-off items stripped, private peers treated as directional, so the numbers hold up under scrutiny.
General approach
  • Uses non-GAAP metrics where relevant; one-off items are stripped before peer comparison (e.g., Adidas FY22 restructuring, Skechers FY24 write-off, Under Armour FY21–23 restructuring charges) so ratios reflect underlying operating performance.
  • Levers are grouped into four transformation buckets: Commercial Excellence, Operational Excellence, SG&A Efficiency, Capital Efficiency. Each is triangulated against Bain experience ranges and peer benchmark data independently, then reconciled to a single recommended range.
  • Recurring EBITDA impact (P&L) and one-off cash release (balance sheet) are sized and shown separately throughout. They are never summed into one headline figure.
  • Peers are adjusted, not identical: private companies (New Balance) are estimated from statutory filings and treated as directional; multi-brand conglomerates (VF Corp) are excluded where brand-level disclosure isn't available.
Metric definitions
MetricDefinitionDirectionality
SG&A ratioSG&A expense as a percentage of net revenue: overhead efficiency relative to scale.Lower is better
Gross marginGross profit as a percentage of net revenue: reflects pricing power and channel/product mix.Higher is better
EBITDA marginEBITDA as a percentage of net revenue: combines gross margin and SG&A efficiency into one measure.Higher is better

Limitations
  • !Nike's DTC mix is structurally higher than a wholesale-weighted peer, which carries more SG&A per dollar of revenue. A mix-adjusted comparison is a first-week refinement.
  • !Revenue-side levers depend on execution assumptions that should be validated before a commitment is made.
  • !New Balance figures are estimated from UK/Germany statutory filings, not full public disclosure, and should be treated as directional only.
