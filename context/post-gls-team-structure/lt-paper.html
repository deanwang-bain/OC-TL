Bringing OpCat in-house after GLS

The goal is for Bain to own Opportunity Catalyst fully. StatusNeo is an external partner and rolls off in stages, so nothing breaks along the way. This paper sets out the three decisions that get us there, the options for each, and what each option leads to.

The team is described by roles and the qualities each role needs, not by which Bain organisation it sits in. That keeps the plan valid through any re-org.

Recommendation

One owner, two dedicated tracks, handed over in three shadowed waves, with the lead engineer hired now

  • 1 · Team shapeOption B: engineering and run, plus data and content, under one Bain ownerKeeps the work that keeps the numbers correct from being crowded out by feature work.
  • 2 · Roll-off paceScenario 2: three waves with reverse shadowingStatusNeo leaves about four weeks after the Industrialisation SOW ends, and the riskiest work moves last.
  • 3 · AnchorStart the Bain lead engineer hire nowEvery option depends on this role being in seat by early January.

If dedicated headcount is the binding constraint, Option C (a small core team plus shared capacity) works with the same waves, at more risk to ownership.

The decisions

Decision 1

What shape is the Bain team that owns OpCat?

Option A

One integrated product team

Every role in one team with one backlog. Simplest, but data and content work competes directly with features.

RecommendedOption B

Two tracks under one owner

Engineering and run, plus data and content. Protects data quality, at the cost of an interface to manage.

Option C

Small core team, shared capacity for the rest

Fewest dedicated hires. Frontend, backend and run come from shared Bain engineering, so ownership is weakest.

Decision 2

How fast does StatusNeo roll off?

Scenario 1

Hard stop at SOW end

Cheapest. The hardest areas get the least handover time.

RecommendedScenario 2

Three waves, reverse shadowing

SOW end plus four weeks. Bain leads each area with StatusNeo watching before it leaves.

Scenario 3

Gated on capability

Several months past SOW end. Lowest risk and highest cost, and ownership is slow to land.

Scenario 4

Platform first

SOW end plus four weeks. Infrastructure moves to the Andromeda platform in December, then the rest.

Decision 3

Who anchors the transition on the Bain side?

RecommendedHire now

Permanent Bain lead engineer, hired now

Joins while StatusNeo is still here, so the knowledge moves to someone who stays.

Appoint internally

Existing senior Bain engineer

Faster to fill if the right person exists. They are likely to be splitting their time with another role.

Defer

Hire after the team is staffed

Spends less early, but the code owner arrives after the people who wrote it have left.

Decision 1 · Outcomes

What each team shape looks like

Every option keeps Bain's product leadership, adds one new Bain lead engineer, and uses the Andromeda platform for infrastructure and CI/CD, which are Bain-owned already. Boxes are roles. Figures are full-time-equivalent estimates for running the product, and new feature work comes on top.

Option A

One integrated product team

What you get
One team, one backlog, one set of rituals. The simplest structure to stand up and to explain.
What it costs
Very different skills share one queue, from agent engineering to benchmarking methodology. Under feature pressure, data refreshes and output review are the first things dropped. That is how a benchmark goes quietly stale while still looking confident.
Transition risk
medium
Choose if
The team stays small, around five people, and the hires are generalists.
RecommendedOption B

Two tracks under one owner

What you get
A data and content track with its own capacity for keeping benchmarks correct and current, and an engineering track for code, releases and incidents. Each track hires for one clear profile.
What it costs
An interface between the tracks. A data fix that needs a code change crosses both, and the lead engineer has to hold them together.
Transition risk
medium
Why we recommend it
For OpCat, the costly failure is a confidently wrong number in front of a client, not downtime. A dedicated data and content track is the structural guard against it.
Option C

Small core team, shared capacity for the rest

What you get
The fewest dedicated hires. Frontend, backend and run capacity flex with demand, which is expected to be bursty around deal cycles.
What it costs
Shared engineers balance OpCat against other priorities, and on-call is held by people who do not live in the product. Ownership is the weakest of the three.
Transition risk
high on run medium elsewhere
Choose if
Dedicated headcount is the binding constraint and a shared engineering pool with real capacity exists.

Decision 2 · Outcomes

What each roll-off scenario leads to

All scenarios begin with the same preparation from now to the SOW start: confirm the team shape, start the lead engineer and data engineer hires, and put Bain people alongside StatusNeo during Sprint 5. Nobody rolls off before the 50+ partner rollout has settled.

Where the dates come from

Only two dates are on record. Industrialisation runs "from ~23 Nov 2026 (proposed, ~12 weeks)" and is "subject to separate SOW", which puts its end around 15 February. The partner rollout is late November or early December.

Everything else is an assumption in this paper. The waves start in January to clear the partner rollout and the year-end break. Mid-March appears only because Scenario 2 adds a four-week advisory tail after the SOW ends. If the SOW moves or is not signed, every date moves with it, which is why the scenarios are stated relative to the SOW end.

What has to be true for a smooth transition

  1. The lead engineer is in seat before wave 1.
  2. A named Bain owner for the calculation engine and for the AI agents before anyone on StatusNeo's side of those leaves.
  3. The missing design standards (security, performance targets, monitoring, interfaces) are written before wave 2. A Bain team cannot run to a standard that does not exist.
  4. Staging loose ends are closed, and the product moves off its proof-of-concept AI account, before infrastructure goes to the platform.
  5. Open vendor and model approvals, including the web search provider, are settled before the agents move, so the Bain team does not inherit unapproved suppliers.
  6. The Industrialisation SOW is signed with a roll-off schedule and notice terms that match the chosen scenario.
