Honda Motor Co., Ltd.

Where is the value, and what would management recognise as its own agenda?

Excluding EV charges, Honda's margin trails the peer median by 1.4 pp because its car business earns almost nothing while motorcycles set records; the car rebuild management has already announced is worth ¥302.7–518.6B of annual EBIT if it closes that gap.

1The situation

In short

  1. Excluding EV-related losses, Honda's EBIT margin is 4.8% against a peer median of 6.2% and a top quartile of 7.1%: 5 of 8, behind Suzuki at 9.9% and Toyota at 7.4%.
  2. The gap sits in cars: motorcycles made a record profit while the automobile business was close to break-even before EV charges, so the value is where management's three-pillar rebuild already points.
  3. Closing the gap to the median is worth ¥302.7–518.6B of annual EBIT (139–238 bps of margin), the same order as the step from today's adjusted profit to management's FY2029 record-profit goal.
  4. Revenue grew 0.5% in the year against a peer median of 1.2%; growth and working capital are shown, not sized.

What the company is facing

  • Motorcycles are the profit engine: record operating profit, led by India and Brazil.[2]
  • The EV reset cancelled three North American models and pushed the group to an operating loss; more EV-related losses are expected in later years.[9]
  • US tariffs weigh on the petrol and hybrid business, and competitiveness in Asia, China above all, has slipped.[6]
  • In the first quarter of FY27 profit more than doubled with no EV charges, helped by a weaker yen.[8]

What management says it is prioritising

Rebuild the automobile business: reallocate resources, strengthen manufacturing, use outside partners

Three pillars to rebuild the automobile business over about three years: strategic reallocation of corporate resources, thorough strengthening of the manufacturing structure, and strategic use of external resources, aiming for record consolidated operating profit of more than ¥1.4 trillion in the year to March 2029.
summaryToshihiro Mibe, President and CEO · 2026-05-14[7]

Move development and production to hybrids, led by North America

Development and production resources move to hybrid cars: 15 next-generation hybrid models globally by the year to March 2030, mainly for North America, rolled out faster than originally planned.
summaryToshihiro Mibe, President and CEO · 2026-05-14[7]
Honda scrapped its goal of EVs at 20% of new-car sales in 2030 and its 2040 all-EV and fuel-cell target, and will invest ¥4.4 trillion in new petrol and hybrid vehicles over three years.
summaryReuters · 2026-05-14[9]

Keep motorcycles and financial services as the stable earners, India first

The motorcycle and financial services businesses maintain solid profitability; India is the largest motorcycle market, with a Honda share of about 28%.
summaryHonda, business briefing · 2026-05-14[7]

Contain the EV reset without leaving EVs

“The company has made a decision to cancel the development and market launch of three EV (electric vehicle) models that had been planned for production in North America.”
Honda, news release · 2026-03-12[6]
Cancelling the North American EVs is 'by no means an indication that Honda is withdrawing from EV business'; EV sales continue in Japan and Asia where they match demand.
summaryToshihiro Mibe, President and CEO · 2026-05-14[11]

Outside view: analysts and industry

Mibe apologised for the loss and shareholders backed the board, as ISS and Glass Lewis advised; he said the car business would have stayed in the red for at least five years had the EV plan gone ahead.
summaryReuters · 2026-06-26[10]

Why the value is real

Cost and margin: EBIT-margin gap
Peers are on their own adjusted or reported basis, as Honda is. The FY2029 record-profit goal implies a step of the same order, and the first quarter of FY27 showed cars back in profit without EV charges.

See quote from Toshihiro Mibe, President and CEO, 2026-05-14[7]

No EV-related losses in the quarter, the automobile business back in profit at about a 5% margin, and the full-year operating-profit forecast raised to ¥650 billion (about ¥1.17 trillion excluding EV-related losses) on a weaker-yen assumption.
summaryHonda, Q1 FY27 results · 2026-08-05[8]

Questions to test with management

  • What automobile margin does the FY2029 record-profit goal assume, and how much comes from hybrids versus manufacturing cost?
  • How much of the remaining EV pullback cost is cash, and when does 'excluding EV losses' stop being the headline measure?
  • Which North American plants and suppliers carry the hybrid shift, and what does tariff mitigation add?
  • Is working capital a lever? Peer balance sheets could not be read here; inventory days against Toyota would settle it.

5Method and sources

  • Every figure comes from a public filing, results release or investor presentation, listed below. Nothing is estimated to fill a gap: a missing value shows as n/a with its reason.
  • Benchmarks are the median and top quartile of the peers that report the metric; the target is never in its own benchmark. Top quartile follows the metric's direction.
  • EBIT is each company's adjusted operating income where it publishes one, and reported operating income otherwise; the peer table names the basis per company. Gross margin is revenue less cost of sales. SG&A is benchmarked as one combined line, because peers split it differently.
  • Sizing closes the gap to the peer median (low) and the top quartile (high), on the latest year's revenue. Growth (reported revenue CAGR) compounds for 3 years to FY29 and converts to EBIT at the stated margin. Cash is one-off and never added to EBIT.
  • Cost is sized as one EBIT-margin gap, because peers' cost lines are not comparable; it is never added to separate cost-of-sales or SG&A buckets. Totals are the sum of the rows shown. This is a sniff test of where value may sit, not a commitment: it has no practitioner experience, case examples or management data behind it.

Limitations

  • Honda's EBIT is its own operating profit excluding EV-related losses (reported: an operating loss). VW, Stellantis, GM and Ford are on their own adjusted measures; Toyota, Hyundai and Suzuki on reported operating profit.
  • Every company includes its captive finance arm; GM and Ford's adjusted EBIT include GM Financial and Ford Credit.
  • Honda's cost of sales and R&D still carry EV impairments, and R&D and finance costs sit in different lines across the set, so cost lines are not charted or sized; one EBIT-margin bucket replaces them.
  • Growth is the latest year against the prior year, because three-year histories could not be read for most peers. Japanese companies report to March 2026, the others to December 2025.
  • Working capital is not sized: too few peer balance sheets could be read from search extracts.
  • Stellantis left out of the Gross margin benchmark: 2025 cost of revenues carries part of the year's unusual charges, which AOI excludes
  • Stellantis left out of the R&D % benchmark: 2025 R&D costs carry part of the year's unusual charges
