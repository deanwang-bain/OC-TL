The Kraft Heinz Company

Where is the value versus packaged-food peers, and what would management recognise as its own agenda?

Kraft Heinz already earns the highest adjusted EBIT margin in its peer set (1 of 8) but ranks 7 of 9 on growth: the value is in returning to peer-level growth, the agenda its new CEO has already set, worth $520–759M of EBIT a year by FY28, plus $621M–$1.7B of one-off working-capital cash led by receivables.

1The situation

In short

  1. Profitability is not the problem. Adjusted EBIT margin of 19.0% is top quartile (1 of 8) against a peer median of 15.3%, and SG&A at 14.7% of revenue is also top quartile.
  2. Growth is. Revenue growth of -2.0% a year compares with a peer median of 1.7% (7 of 9). Growing at the median instead is worth $520–695M of EBIT a year by FY28, nearly all of the $520–759M prize.
  3. Cost of sales is at par. Gross margin of 33.3% is above median; the gap to the top quartile is only up to $63.6M.
  4. Cash sits in receivables and inventory. DSO of 33 days against a peer median of 25 days drives most of the $621M–$1.7B one-off cash; payables are already above median.

What the company is facing

  • A new CEO, Steve Cahillane, took over in January 2026 and paused the planned split into two companies to put every resource behind the operating plan.[5]
  • The problem is volume, not price: volume/mix drove the organic sales decline, and share losses narrowed in 2026 but have not reversed.[4]
  • Non-cash impairments in 2025 and again in the second quarter of 2026 have written down the carrying value of the brand portfolio.[4]
  • The shareholder base is shifting: Berkshire Hathaway registered its whole stake for possible sale, and several analysts remain cautious on the turnaround.[8]

Why the value is real

Commercial: revenue growth
Peer growth is reported, not organic: Campbell's and Smucker's include acquisitions and Mondelez includes emerging-market pricing, while General Mills and Conagra are held back by divestitures. Sector growth is expected to stay low, so treat the top-quartile end as a stretch.

See quote from TD Cowen and CFRA, as reported, 2026-02-17[10]

Operations: cost of sales
Kraft Heinz is already above the peer median, so the range is the gap to the top quartile only. Mondelez and Hershey margins are depressed by cocoa and hedging, Hormel's by its protein mix.
Working capital
Receivables are the cleanest lever, with Kraft Heinz in the bottom quartile on DSO. The payables leg assumes stretching terms further on top of supplier-finance programmes already in place, so the low end is the more credible figure.

Questions to test with management

  • Restate the growth gap on organic sales, removing acquisitions, divestitures, 53rd weeks and hyperinflation pricing, before using the top-quartile end.
  • Track whether the reinvestment is turning volume and share by category and channel on the path to FY28.
  • Compare customer terms, any receivable-sale programmes and inventory days by category with peers to firm up the cash range.
  • Ask how the impairments and the paused split shape portfolio choices: which brands to fix, fund or exit.

5Method and sources

  • Every figure comes from a public filing, results release or investor presentation, listed below. Nothing is estimated to fill a gap: a missing value shows as n/a with its reason.
  • Benchmarks are the median and top quartile of the peers that report the metric; the target is never in its own benchmark. Top quartile follows the metric's direction.
  • EBIT is each company's own adjusted operating income, from its non-GAAP reconciliation; definitions differ and are noted where they matter. Gross margin is revenue less cost of sales. SG&A is benchmarked as one combined line, because peers split it differently.
  • Sizing closes the gap to the peer median (low) and the top quartile (high), on the latest year's revenue. Growth (reported revenue CAGR) compounds for 3 years to FY28 and converts to EBIT at the stated margin. Cash is one-off and never added to EBIT.
  • Totals are the sum of the rows shown. This is a sniff test of where value may sit, not a commitment: it has no practitioner experience, case examples or management data behind it.

Limitations

  • EBIT is each company's own adjusted operating income from its non-GAAP reconciliation, because impairments distort reported figures (Kraft Heinz reports an operating loss). Definitions differ; Smucker's excludes amortization, which flatters its margin.
  • Conagra publishes an adjusted operating margin but no dollar figure in the sources reachable here, and has no operating-income line, so it is n/a on EBIT margin.
  • Growth is reported, not organic, over each company's latest three fiscal years, so windows differ; Kraft Heinz's base year and General Mills' and Conagra's latest years include a 53rd week.
  • Mondelez and Hershey cost of sales include commodity mark-to-market losses in a record cocoa year; Hormel is a protein processor with a structurally lower gross margin. Kraft Heinz and McCormick hold supplier-finance balances inside trade payables; Conagra's payables line includes other payables.
