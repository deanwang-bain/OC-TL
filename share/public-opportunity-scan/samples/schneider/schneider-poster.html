Schneider Electric SE

Where is the value, and what would management recognise as its own agenda?

Schneider out-grows its peers but converts less of that growth into margin: closing the adjusted EBITA margin gap to the peer median and top quartile is worth €451M–€1.4B a year, the range management's own 2030 margin target already reaches into, plus up to €1.3–2.2B of one-off cash if the receivables gap is real.

1The situation

In short

  1. Growth is not the gap: organic growth of 8.9% ranks 1 of 7 against a peer median of 5.2%, and 2026 guidance has been raised twice.
  2. Margin is: adjusted EBITA margin of 18.7% ranks 6 of 7, 1.1 pp below the peer median of 19.9%; closing to median and top quartile is worth €451M–€1.4B of EBIT (112–345 bps).
  3. Where the margin gap sits depends on the comparator: against Eaton and Hubbell it is support functions (SG&A 19.9% of revenue against a median of 17.8%); against Legrand and Emerson it is gross margin (rank 4 of 7); and Industrial Automation earns well below the group.
  4. Cash: receivables run 89 days against 77 days for peers, worth €1.3–2.2B one-off if the gap is real, and cash matters more once PTC adds new debt.

What the company is facing

  • Revenue reached €40.2B in FY25, led by Energy Management and data centres; Industrial Automation grew far more slowly.[1]
  • Pricing rose in North America with tariffs while China stayed deflationary.[11]
  • Guidance for 2026 has been raised twice, on data-centre demand and a record backlog.[8]
  • In October 2026 Schneider agreed to buy PTC, its largest deal ever, funded mostly with new debt; the shares fell on the news.[10]

What management says it is prioritising

Grow organically ahead of the market to 2030, led by data centres and infrastructure

Targets for 2026 to 2030: organic revenue growth of 7% to 10% a year, an adjusted EBITA margin about 250 bps higher by 2030, cash conversion around 100%, and up to €3.5 billion of buybacks.
summarySchneider Electric, Capital Markets Day, as reported by Reuters · 2025-12-11[6]
Raised 2026 targets for the second time, to 12% to 14% organic revenue growth and 18% to 21% organic adjusted EBITA growth; data-centre demand grew at triple digits in the quarter, with a record backlog and visibility into 2027 and 2028.
summaryOlivier Blum, CEO · 2026-10-16[8]

Lift the margin through pricing, industrial productivity and support costs growing slower than sales

Named two main drivers of gross margin: pricing and industrial productivity.
summarySchneider Electric management, H1 2026 results call · 2026-07-30[7]
Support function costs rose 4.2% organically in 2025 while revenue grew 8.9% organically; support function costs were 23.3% of sales.
summarySchneider Electric, FY2025 results presentation · 2026-02-26[2]

Restore Industrial Automation margins under a dedicated recovery plan

“The adjusted EBITA in industrial automation also improved year-over-year by 50 basis points organic as we implement the margin recovery plan there to reach 18% by 2028.”
Schneider Electric management, H1 2026 results call (speaker not named in the extract) · 2026-07-30[7]

Build an industrial software and AI platform with PTC

Agreed to buy PTC for about $22.6 billion in cash, calling the combination the industry's most complete software and AI powerhouse; targets about €250 million of run-rate cost savings by year three and about €800 million of revenue synergies, with closing expected by Q3 2027.
summaryOlivier Blum, CEO · 2026-10-05[10]

Outside view: analysts and industry

RBC: the deal multiples are reasonable, but the deal raises exposure to AI concerns over industrial software and the integration will be complicated. Jefferies: the fears that allowed a decade-low price could weigh on the shares after the deal.

Shares fell about 8% on the announcement, erasing close to €15 billion of market value; the deal adds €16 to 17 billion of new debt and buybacks are paused.

Questions to test with management

  • How much of the margin gap to Legrand, Hubbell and Emerson is mix (software, premium products, acquisition accounting) and how much is support-function cost?
  • What does the Industrial Automation recovery plan add to group margin by 2028, and which levers carry it?
  • What support-function cost ratio does the 2030 margin ambition assume, before and after PTC?
  • With PTC's debt and paused buybacks, does receivables collection become part of the cash plan?

5Method and sources

  • Every figure comes from a public filing, results release or investor presentation, listed below. Nothing is estimated to fill a gap: a missing value shows as n/a with its reason.
  • Benchmarks are the median and top quartile of the peers that report the metric; the target is never in its own benchmark. Top quartile follows the metric's direction.
  • EBIT is each company's adjusted operating income where it publishes one, and reported operating income otherwise; the peer table names the basis per company. Gross margin is revenue less cost of sales. SG&A is benchmarked as one combined line, because peers split it differently.
  • Sizing closes the gap to the peer median (low) and the top quartile (high), on the latest year's revenue. Growth (organic, as each company reports it) compounds for 3 years to FY28 and converts to EBIT at the stated margin. Cash is one-off and never added to EBIT.
  • Cost is sized as one EBIT-margin gap, because peers' cost lines are not comparable; it is never added to separate cost-of-sales or SG&A buckets. Totals are the sum of the rows shown. This is a sniff test of where value may sit, not a commitment: it has no practitioner experience, case examples or management data behind it.

Limitations

  • EBIT basis is mixed. The rule is to switch to adjusted figures when one-offs distort the latest year, and they do: Rockwell carries a goodwill impairment and other charges, Mitsubishi Electric an early-retirement programme, Schneider doubled restructuring. Each company is on its own adjusted measure (EBITA-type for Schneider, ABB and Emerson; adjusted operating profit for Legrand, Hubbell and Mitsubishi Electric); Eaton publishes none at group level and stays on reported pretax income plus net interest.
  • Measures that exclude acquisition amortisation flatter acquisitive companies, Emerson most; on reported EBIT Schneider's margin is lower (see the peer table).
  • Siemens is not in the set: its comparable businesses are segments of a group that also holds Mobility, Healthineers and Financial Services, with no segment cost of sales, SG&A, working capital or combined organic growth.
  • Cost and margin are sized on the EBIT-margin gap, not on gross margin and SG&A separately: the two lines trade off across business models, R&D sits in different lines (Schneider books part of it in cost of sales; Rockwell moved engineering out of cost of sales), and SG&A is available for only four peers.
  • The working-capital benchmark rests on four peers; Schneider's year-end balances are the restated comparatives in its H1 2026 accounts.
  • Fiscal years differ (Rockwell and Emerson to September, Mitsubishi Electric to March); USD revenue is converted at the calendar-2025 average; growth is each company's own organic, comparable or underlying measure. No valuation or TSR was collected.
  • Rockwell left out of the EBIT margin benchmark: Rockwell: only a segment-level margin is published (it leaves out corporate costs), and the reported FY2025 result carries a goodwill impairment and other one-off charges, so it is shown but kept out of the EBIT-margin benchmark
